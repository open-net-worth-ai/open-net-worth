# Key invariants — full text

> **On-demand reference.** Not loaded into every turn — read it when you touch this area.
> Decisions and their reasoning live in [`docs/adr/`](adr/); this file states current truth.

`CLAUDE.md` carries the one-line form of each. This file is the long form with the reasoning and the scar that produced it.

- **"Overdue" and "due in the next 30 days" have ONE definition**, `commitments.due_summary()`: an OBLIGATION is money out of an active commitment (an unpaid installment, or a `pending` action), whatever the commitment's kind — an auto-pay debit and a goal's set-aside are obligations; a funding draw is money IN and never is. Overdue = an obligation dated before today; due soon = today through today + 30, inclusive. A planned funding inflow past its date is `funding_overdue`, reported apart and never counted in the overdue figure. A funding row that has ARRIVED (`commitments.funding_arrived`: a `realized_txn_id` or `status == "done"`, the twin of `_is_paid`) is neither late nor coming: `upcoming_events`, `funding_source_overdue` and `projected_events` all skip it, so it is never projected a second time (the per-commitment `_plan_fidelity_events` trace keeps it: it starts from $0, so the arrived money is the plan's own). The Dashboard's Next-30-days card (`/api/commitments/summary`), the Commitments masthead's "Due in 30 days", its attention lane's overdue items and the daily briefing all read it; none re-derives it (persona QA round 5: 8 vs 6 overdue, "Due in 30 days S$0.00" beside S$7,683 going out). The same card lists recurring INCOME landing in the window (`recurring_baseline.upcoming_income`: matured or confirmed detected streams, occurrences after today; declared streams were retired, ADR `2026-10-16-declared-recurring-streams-are-retired`) under a separate "coming in" total. ADR `2026-10-14-one-definition-per-fact-across-screens`.
- **The runway is ONE function**, `cashflow.runway_from_projection` / `cash_runway()` (cash ÷ the projection's `monthly_expenses_avg`, growth-compounded): the Cash Flow hero, the Dashboard runway widget and the "Thin cash buffer" flag read it. The flag once divided by a plain average of all spending (one-offs included) and quoted 1.9 months beside Cash Flow's 2.1.
- **A spending-based figure names the cards it cannot see.** `card_coverage.cards_without_activity(from, to)` lists open credit cards with no purchase (expense) in the figure's own period; the savings rate (`reports.savings.cards_missing_spending`), the burn/runway (`cards_missing_spending` on the projection) carry it and their screens say "Card spending not included yet". Starting Balances and card payments are not activity.
- **The funding verdict is ONE cache entry on the client.** The Dashboard's "Funding shortfall" flag and the Commitments masthead both read `commitmentsCoverageKey(12)` (`/api/commitments/coverage?months=12`) through `useCommitmentsCoverageRead`; `/api/commitments/summary` no longer carries coverage. Two independent fetches, an FX refresh apart, once showed S$13,792.08 and S$13,792.95.
- **The daily briefing's cached note is valid only for the book structure it was written against.** `briefing_context.book_fingerprint` (accounts and types, envelope-to-category links, any assignment, position count, overdue/due counts, budget engine) is stored with the note; a different fingerprint regenerates it. Balances and prices are deliberately not in it. "Can cash absorb the card" is answered only by Ready to Assign (`cash_availability_lines`), never by account balances; today's weakest/strongest holding comes from the FULL ranking (`movers_lines`), not the largest dozen shown.
- `category` is OPTIONAL — expense/income/investment/transfer may carry `None` (rendered "Uncategorized"); there is NO forced `uncategorized` sink, and `create_transaction` RAISES `ValidationError` on an unknown category (never silently coerces)
- **Category ids are OPAQUE** (`cat_<12hex>`, minted via `core.ids.new_id("cat")`; built-in seeds carry a stable hardcoded id, e.g. `cat_food` — see `models/category_defs.py`) — never a slug/key derived from the label (`normalize_key` is GONE). `label` is the only human-readable field. `services.categories.resolve_category_ref(ref, rows=None)` resolves a caller-supplied id OR label (case-insensitive exact match) to the stored id — wired at the ledger write choke (`create_transaction`/`bulk_create_transactions`/split validation), CSV/QIF import, and the `get_transactions` read filter, since those are the paths most likely to receive a raw label instead of a pre-looked-up id. `rename_category(key, new_label=None, merge_into=None)` never changes a category's own id: **relabel** (id unchanged, nothing else re-keyed) or **merge** (fold `key`'s data into an existing different `merge_into` id, then delete `key`'s row) — there is no more "rename A to B" re-key. `create_category(label, kind, color, group)` takes no caller-supplied key. Categories carry no taxability field; taxability is transaction-level only. See `docs/adr/2026-07-05-category-opaque-ids.md` and `docs/adr/2026-09-21-remove-category-taxability.md`.
- **Account refs resolve by NAME too** (ADR `2026-07-19-account-refs-resolve-by-name`) — `services.accounts.resolve_account_ref(ref, rows=None)` is the account twin of `resolve_category_ref`, wired at the ledger write choke (`create_transaction`/`bulk_create_transactions` via `_resolve_account_refs`, which runs BEFORE `matching.check` since the L0 dedup key includes the account id), the bulk `to_account_id` move, the structured-import path (`import_common.resolve_account`), and the AI tool validators that used to refuse a name BEFORE the choke ever saw it (`ai/tools/_shared._validate_account_id`, `ai/tools/imports._resolve_account`, `ai/tools/statements._resolve_account_or_error`). Ladder: exact id → itself; exact name (case-insensitive) among NON-ARCHIVED; exact name among archived (only when no live account claims it). Resolution is LAZY and store-identity-safe: each site checks its OWN store handle with a cheap `find_by_id` first (`_unresolved_account_fields`) and reads the full store ONLY when a non-id ref is actually present — an id-only write pays nothing, and the resolver is always handed the caller's rows (`rows=`) so it can never disagree with the existence check that runs right after it. **Ambiguity RAISES `ValidationError` naming every candidate** (currency/platform/archived/id) — the ONE deliberate divergence from `resolve_category_ref`, which returns None: for a category "unresolved" degrades to Uncategorized (safe), but for an account it would report "no such account" for a name that matches two, and a wrong account is a wrong balance. Archived accounts match (a leg BY ID has always accepted one) but NEVER shadow a live namesake. Matching is EXACT — never fuzzy; fuzzy is for the ERROR (`core.errors.recovery_hint` names the closest real names). Motivated by measured data, not inspection: account-ref resolution was ~20% of all tool errors across 289 real sessions. KNOWN GAP (pinned as characterization, not fixed): `_validate_legs` existence-checks both legs of a transfer but neither leg of an expense/income/investment.
- Kinds and categories are DISJOINT namespaces: kind names (`expense|income|transfer|adjustment|investment`) can never be stored as a transaction's category value (`_validate_reserved_category` guard at the write choke refuses the raw literal; `_KIND_NAME_KEYS` is the shared reserved-word constant, still consulted by `payee_registry`) — with opaque ids this is now ALSO structurally impossible at the category-store level (a minted id can never literally equal a kind name), so `create_category`/`rename_category` no longer check it themselves; `adjustment` rows carry `category=None` (stripped on write, always); every other kind (`expense|income|investment|transfer`) carries an OPTIONAL user-chosen category (NOT stripped) — a transfer's category validates exactly like an expense's (owner decision 2026-07-03, retiring the old "transfers carry category=None" invariant now that the envelope⊥category split makes category an inert label; `planning._activity_by_category`'s on-budget-leg XOR fold decides what counts, never the write path — see `docs/adr/2026-07-03-categorized-transfers.md`)
- `kind=transfer` REQUIRES both legs: `from_account_id` + `to_account_id`, distinct, both existing accounts (archived OK) — enforced in `_validate_legs` at the create/update choke points
- **A transfer's figure and its unit belong to the SAME leg** (ADR `2026-08-23-a-transfer-figure-and-its-unit-belong-to-the-same-leg`). On the AI tool surface `ai/tools/_shared.py::_txn_data_from_ai` maps ONE anchor-based shape (`account_id` + `direction` + a counterparty key) onto the symmetric two-leg schema under a single rule: **`amount`/`currency` describe the SOURCE leg, `to_amount`/`to_currency` the DESTINATION leg**, and the anchor decides only WHICH account sits on which side — never how the caller's figure and its unit are paired. It used to be three branches, and two of them split that pair: the NUMBER went to the source leg while the caller's UNIT went to the destination leg, so the source leg silently took its own ACCOUNT's currency. That is invisible to every downstream guard by construction — `_normalise_leg` compares the leg's currency against its account's, sees them equal, skips FX as a no-op, and stamps no `original_amount`, which is the exact field `ai/tools/ledger.py::_currency_conversion_warning` keys off. Observed bug (2026-08-23): a ₫250,000,000 loan repayment booked as **S$250,000,000** on an SGD receivable (~20,600x), surfacing only as an absurd net-worth figure a reviewer happened to notice. A destination amount the caller did NOT supply is derived by `_counterpart_amount` — the same number when both legs share a currency (exact, no FX call, the case that default was written for), otherwise converted at today's rate via `to_base`, and **never the raw number carried across a currency boundary and relabelled**. Consequence to know before reaching for it: in the credit form the source leg is FX-DERIVED, so a caller pinning both legs exactly must use the debit form (`account_id` = source, `to_amount`/`to_currency` = destination) — `to_currency` no longer doubles as the source leg's currency the way two of the old branches let it. This is the THIRD scar on this mapping: FIX A (Meridian Bank XLSX, an omitted currency defaulting to base) and FIX A2 (Anthropic/Beacon Bank, an omitted currency defaulting to the account's) both hardened the currency a caller STATES; neither covered the currency the mapping ASSIGNS on the caller's behalf. All three are pinned together in `tests/test_currency_atomic.py`
- **A transfer whose legs imply an impossible exchange rate is REFUSED at the choke** (ADR `2026-08-23-an-impossible-transfer-rate-is-refused-at-the-choke`). The backstop half of the invariant above, which fixes only the ONE caller that produced the incident. `services.ledger._assert_transfer_rate_plausible`, called from `resolve_transaction_row` AFTER both `_normalise_leg` calls, compares the rate the two legs imply (`to_amount/from_amount`) against `core.fx.known_rate(from_currency, to_currency)` and raises `ValidationError` when they differ by more than `_TRANSFER_RATE_SANITY_FACTOR` (10x), naming both figures, both rates, and the amount the leg should have carried. Three design points that are load-bearing, each with its own pinned test: (1) **a RATE comparison, not the 'equal amounts in different currencies' test the predecessor ADR proposed** — equality catches only an EXACT copy (copy-then-round, or a conversion done with a rate wrong by 1000x, walks past it) and is permanently wrong for a genuinely 1:1 pair, which a USD↔USDC wallet transfer really is; (2) **ignorance never refuses** — `known_rate` is NEW precisely because `to_base` cannot express this: `to_base` passes an unknown pair through 1:1 by deliberate fail-soft design, making its result identical for 'the rate is 1.0' and 'this currency is not in the table', so `known_rate` returns None for the latter and the guard declines to judge rather than refuse a write for a reason the user cannot act on; (3) **in the RESOLUTION half, not `create_transaction`** — a preview must refuse too, or a confirm card renders a row the write then rejects — and AFTER normalisation, since the rate between two legs is only meaningful once each is in its own account's currency (`_validate_legs` runs too early, on whatever currencies the caller stated). 10x is deliberately loose: the bug class is 1,000x–20,000x while the honest error sources (bank spread, stale cache, a historical transfer entered at its own day's rate) are percentages, occasionally a factor of two on a long-depreciating pair. The guard is only as good as the rates table — an empty cache means it silently does not fire, so it is a backstop, never a substitute for pairing a figure with its unit correctly. Pinned by `tests/test_transfer_rate_sanity.py`. Companion: `_txn_data_from_ai(derived_out=…)` fills when a destination leg was DERIVED across a currency boundary, surfaced by `add_transactions` as a `leg_amount_derived` warning — `_currency_conversion_warning` structurally cannot see it, since it reads `original_amount` and `_resolve_original` returns `(None, None)` for a transfer by design. It matters where a transfer settles a balance to zero: two tranches at today's rate do not sum to the amount actually owed, and the account ends at '$2.40 still owed'
- **Equal amounts across a KNOWN, unequal-rate pair are REFUSED; an unknown pair is WARNED** (ADR `2026-09-02-equal-amounts-across-a-known-unequal-rate-pair-are-refused`). Sibling of the 10x guard above, which cannot see the miss a model makes most: the right rate in its own reasoning, never multiplied, landing on an implied 1:1 that is nowhere near 10x off on an ordinary fiat pair (the 2026-08-31 broker-statement automation). `services.ledger._assert_transfer_amount_converted`, called from `resolve_transaction_row` AFTER `_assert_transfer_rate_plausible`, raises `ImplausibleTransferAmountError` (409, `implausible_transfer_amount`) when the two legs carry the exact same number (2 dp) in different currencies AND `core.fx.known_rate` is not None AND that rate is more than `_TRANSFER_ONE_TO_ONE_TOLERANCE` (2%, a stablecoin's own wobble) from 1.0 — naming both figures, the rate, and the amount the destination leg should have carried. The 2026-08-23 ADR rejected an equality test because it is permanently wrong for a genuine 1:1 pair; this one is scoped to fire ONLY when `known_rate` says the pair is NOT 1:1, so that blind spot does not exist and the 10x decision stands in full. Three load-bearing points, each pinned: (1) **ignorance warns, never refuses** — a None rate (every crypto pair; the FX cache holds only Yahoo fiat crosses) declines to judge, and `add_transactions` instead appends a non-blocking `unverified_equal_amount_transfer` warning (single and bulk, tagged per `txn_id`) telling the agent to verify and repair via `update_transaction`; once the pair becomes known the same shape is refused, no configuration; (2) **one bypass, one name at every layer** — `force_transfer_rate` on the tool, on `create_transaction`/`bulk_create_transactions`/`preview_create`/`resolve_transaction_row`, bypassing ONLY this guard (never the 10x one) and never altering the amounts, deliberately unlike the `allow_duplicate` → `force` rename; (3) **an edit re-judges only the legs it changes** — `update_transaction` rebuilds with `force_transfer_rate=not _leg_changed`, because unlike the 10x guard this one has a bypass, so a forced parity row exists in the store and re-judging it on a note/category edit would refuse a shape the store already holds. Pinned by `tests/test_transfer_rate_sanity.py` (guard, bypass, parity band, edit path) and `tests/test_currency_atomic.py` (unknown-pair warning).
- **An account's opening balance is ONE fact: `account["opening_txn_id"]`** (ADR `2026-07-31-opening-balance-is-one-fact`, completing ADR `2026-07-28-starting-balance-single-surface`). The pointer names that account's single `kind="adjustment"`, `cleared=True`, `category=None`, `description="Starting Balance"` transaction. The **amount** is that row's signed leg (positive→to-leg, negative→from-leg; `0.0` is a LEGAL opening, booked as a zero to-leg — "my balance was zero when tracking started"), and the **tracking-start cutoff** is that row's `date`. `null` = no tracking start; every leg counts. **Deleted from storage**: `Account.initial_balance`, `Account.initial_balance_date`, the row's `source: "opening"` column, and its `external_refs: [{"source": "opening", "ref": <account_id>}]` entry — the row stores `source: "manual"` (honest: the opening is a number the user asserted by hand) and no refs. One marker, one read path: **no fallback, no dual read, no absent-field tolerance anywhere**; a one-shot destructive migration (`backend/scripts/migrate_opening_txn_pointer.py`) put the live book into this shape and the code does not know the old one existed. The scars this closes: (1) the same fact lived in FOUR places, and `set_account_opening` wrote the account's cutoff only when `as_of` was truthy while ALWAYS dating the row — so an `as_of=None` call left a stale cutoff behind a re-dated row, and `is_before_tracking_start`'s strict `<` then made the opening **exclude ITSELF from its own fold and report a balance of 0** on a ₫9.86B account (live, twice). The cutoff is now READ OFF the row, so the drift is *unrepresentable*, not merely guarded. (2) `amount == 0` used to soft-DELETE the row and leave the cutoff standing behind nothing — which is how 8 live accounts came to hold a tracking start with no opening and one checking account accumulated FOUR tombstones all bearing the same `("opening", account_id)` pair, proving that pair could never express uniqueness (`by_external_ref` filters `deleted`, so each zero call tombstoned and each nonzero call minted afresh). A scalar pointer gets uniqueness from the SCHEMA. Write side: `ledger.set_account_opening(account_id, amount, as_of)` is the ONE writer — it mints or patches the row IN PLACE (same id, so the pointer never moves) and stamps the pointer; every account create requires `initial_balance` (zero is valid and still mints today’s row), and `services.accounts.create_account` commits the account, row, pointer, and audit in one DB transaction. Read side: `opening_amount(account)` / `opening_date(account)` / `cutoffs_by_account(accts, txns)` (the loop-hoisted map every multi-account fold uses — the only shape that avoids an O(accounts × txns) index rebuild) / `opening_txn_ids()` (the O(1) "is this row an opening?" set, replacing `primary_source(row) == "opening"` at every consumer: the update/delete/cleared guards, the store's sweep exemption, the RTA `starting_balances` bucket, and the register's display projection via `display_source(..., is_opening=True)`) / `opening_index(txns, accts)`. **The fold takes the cutoff as an ARGUMENT** — `account_balance(account, txns, opening_date)`, likewise `cleared_account_balance` and `account_balance_as_of` — and `is_before_tracking_start(leg_date, opening_date)` is a pure date comparison with no account dict and no field lookup, so a fold site that forgets the cutoff is a TypeError the type checker catches rather than a `None` that silently counts pre-start legs. There is no separate opening seed in the fold: the row IS a leg, dated exactly at the cutoff, and the strict `<` always counts it. **A dangling pointer RAISES `DataIntegrityError`** naming the account and the id — it never degrades to `None`, because that would silently un-curtain the account's pre-start history and MOVE its balance (a loud outage is fixable; a wrong net worth is not). That holds for every READ; the WRITE choke is the one exception, and deliberately so (ADR `2026-08-01-referential-file-pairs-revert-together`): `set_account_opening` resolves the row it patches through `_opening_row_to_patch`, which treats a missing/deleted target as "no row" and re-mints — a repair surface that raised on the very pointer it exists to fix left a hand-edit of `accounts.json` as the only way back. `opening_for_edit(account)` is the same tolerant resolve exposed for edits that supply only the amount or only the date (the REST PUT and `update_accounts` fill the omitted field from it). Creating the state is separately prevented: `set_account_opening` writes `accounts` and `transactions` together through ONE `locked_unit_of_work` transaction, so row-level `write_log` captures both rows in the SAME checkpoint and `restore_checkpoint` (model-callable) cannot revert one out from under the other. (Historical: in the JsonStore era this was file-level — `accounts.json` and `transactions.json` were ONE checkpoint unit via `ai.checkpoint._COMPANIONS` — superseded by Plan 3's DB cutover, and `_COMPANIONS` is now permanently empty; ADR `2026-08-01-referential-file-pairs-revert-together`.) The wire contract did NOT move: `initial_balance`/`initial_balance_date` remain input parameters on `POST/PUT /api/accounts` and the AI account tools, and remain DERIVED fields on `account_with_balance()` responses. `opening_txn_id` is SERVER-OWNED — off `AccountCreate`/`AccountUpdate` and `_ACCOUNT_UPDATE_FIELDS`, and explicitly refused by `services.accounts.assert_no_server_owned_fields`, since a caller-settable pointer hands one account another account's balance. One deliberate weakening, named: the filter-sweep exemption moved from an unconditional in-store check to a `protected_ids` parameter the service choke supplies (a store must not read another store) — so on both `select_where` and `bulk_delete_where` it is **keyword-only with NO default**, making a forgotten exemption a TypeError rather than a silent sweep, backed by an AST test that pins them to exactly ONE caller and rejects an alias or `getattr` reach (scanning `backend/` **and** repo-root `scripts/`).
- **An external id links to at most one account** — `Account.external_ids: [{source, ref, name, mask, linked_at}]` (RFC `connectors-as-data-sources` §D, replacing the nine provider config stores' own `account_map` blobs). A `(source, ref)` pair already held by a DIFFERENT account is refused (`services.accounts.assert_external_ids_unique`, called from `create_account` and the `update_account` choke) — `ValidationError` names the holding account. `connected` is DERIVED (`bool(external_ids)`, stamped on every `account_with_balance` read), never independently writable or trusted — a pre-RFC row's stored `connected` value is dead weight nothing reads. Linking fills `last_four`/`platform` from the entry's `mask`/`institution` only when they're currently blank.
- `delete_account` is a three-way choke (ADR `2026-09-06-force-delete-account-hard-removes-its-cascaded-transactions`): no LIVE references → delete outright; the ONLY LIVE reference is the account's own opening (Starting Balance) transaction → delete both, atomically, no `force` needed; other LIVE references → REFUSED (409, naming the count, any OTHER account a shared transfer also touches, and `force=true`) unless `force=True`, which cascades — soft-deletes every referencing transaction (audit + rejected-identities bookkeeping intact, mirroring `bulk_delete_transactions`, though the audit event carries the FULL row as `before` since nothing survives). ALL FOUR of those cases fall through to ONE single code path that then HARD-deletes EVERY row (live or already soft-deleted, re-queried fresh) referencing the account so the `ON DELETE RESTRICT` foreign key is satisfied, then deletes the account, all atomically — there is deliberately no early-return special case for "no live references", since a fully soft-deleted account (`delete_transactions_where` then `delete_account`, or an import-batch rollback) still has rows physically present; a residual `IntegrityError` past that sweep still surfaces as a typed `ConflictError`, never a raw 500. `DELETE /api/accounts/{id}?force=true` is the wire surface (409 body carries structured `transaction_count`/`other_accounts`, never prose-parsed); the frontend only ever sends `force=true` after a SECOND explicit user confirmation past the first attempt's 409, never auto-escalated. EVERY path (including "no references") is refused OUTRIGHT first if the account is still tied to net worth through a no-DB-FK reference — `owned_assets.loan_account_id`, a live position, a live lot, or an open option contract — as its OWN structured 409 shape (`error: "account_net_worth_linked"`, never conflated with the transaction-count shape, since `force=true` can't fix any of these); deliberately NOT extended to commitments/income_events/statements_meta/import_batches (a named, bounded gap, not an oversight). Commitment links (`linked_account_id`, a funding source's `ref_account_id`) are instead CLEARED in the delete's own unit of work (2026-10-13 amendment), never left pointing at a deleted account.
- **The Starting Balance transaction is protected against EVERY destructive operation, merge included** — it is the account's opening balance (ledger-tracking-start Phase 2 relocated the `initial_balance` scalar into this real `adjustment` row; `opening_amount` finds it by its `("opening", <account id>)` external-ref pair, and `account_with_balance` derives the public `initial_balance` from that). Four guards, all keyed off the ONE helper `ledger._is_opening_txn` so the lookup can change in one place: **delete** refused (`bulk_delete_transactions`, pre-scanned so the refusal is atomic — no partial delete of the rest of the batch); **uncleared** refused (`set_transaction_cleared` — it must stay cleared or `cleared_account_balance` drops and reconcile misfires); **edit** narrowed to amount + date routed through `set_account_opening` (`_apply_opening_txn_edit`, id preserved, foreign `external_refs` refused, single leg enforced — ADR `2026-07-28-starting-balance-single-surface`); and **merge** refused from EITHER side (`ledger.assert_merge_allowed`, ConflictError → 409). Merge was the last hole and the worst: as the ABSORBED row it is tombstoned *and* hands its opening pair to the canonical, so `opening_amount` then reads a FOREIGN row's leg — reproduced at ₫5,000,000,000 → ₫42.00 with the account balance collapsing to 0, silently. As the CANONICAL it survives but swallows the other row's provenance (a re-import of that real statement line becomes a no-op at L0) and its own leg can be rewritten under it by `_maybe_adopt_settled_amounts` (an opening row reads as "feed-grade") or rebuilt into a two-leg transfer by `become_transfer`. Refused from BOTH sides because which row the dedup engine names canonical is not the user's choice — a one-sided guard would be bypassable by argument order. **`override=True` does NOT bypass it**: `override` exists to force past the matching engine's same-event/same-feed heuristics (F23a), not to license data destruction, and the delete guard it mirrors has no bypass either. The guard is asserted at the ledger choke AND re-asserted in `match_review.merge_transactions_preserving_fields`, which PATCHES the canonical with the absorbed row's memo/tags before calling the choke — guarding only at the choke would refuse the merge yet still have stamped the opening row. Reachable from the dedup review queue's accept, the `merge_transactions` AI tool, and `POST /api/matches/merge`; `apply_import_transfer_pair_merge` is a second door but already shut, since it routes through `update_transaction`'s opening branch. Pinned by `tests/test_starting_balance_single_surface.py`. **One deliberate, narrow exception**: `delete_account` (ADR `2026-09-06-force-delete-account-hard-removes-its-cascaded-transactions`) DOES delete the opening row — but only together with its OWN account, in the SAME atomic write, never on its own and never via `bulk_delete_transactions`/`merge_transactions`/`set_transaction_cleared`, which still refuse it exactly as above.
- `STRUCTURAL_KEYS = frozenset({"split"})` — the split sentinel is the ONLY structural category (cannot be renamed/deleted/hidden) and the ONE category id that is a literal string rather than an opaque `cat_xxx` (it's a marker, not a real category); every other category, including former built-ins, is pure data and fully user-deletable
- Transaction audit: every create/update/delete emits to the `txn_audit` table via `services/audit.py`; context flows via `core/audit_context.py` ContextVar; `_ui_audit_scope` dep MUST be `async`. A spend rule that DECIDES the stored category at the create choke (override of a caller category, or fill of a blank) stamps sparse `rule_applied` provenance `{rule_id, rule_name (at write time), category_from, category_to}` on the create event — rules run INSIDE `create_transaction`, so without this the override is invisible (the row is simply "born" with the rule's category; live incident 2026-07-17); `audit.activity()` surfaces it as a note + category diff on the drawer's created item (ADR `2026-07-17-rule-override-audit-provenance`)
- Category precedence at `create_transaction`: explicit > rule > payee-default > `None` (Uncategorized — no sink); an explicit unknown category is REJECTED, not coerced
- Payee normalization single source of truth: `services/payees._normalize_payee()`
- **The dedup engine's identity invariant tests the EMITTER, not the namespace** (ADR `2026-09-02-the-emitter-is-the-unit-of-identity-not-the-namespace`, superseding `2026-08-27-a-transfer-has-two-counterparties-and-both-notify`). F03 says two rows carrying distinct authoritative refs under one `external_source` are two real events — its premise is that one namespace is one issuer's event feed. That holds for `ofx`/`simplefin` (one connection, one id per event) and is FALSE for `email`, which carries every sender the book has ever received: a Message-ID is authoritative for the EMAIL, not the PAYMENT, so one charge announced by the bank AND by the merchant is two ids for one event. `matching._same_source_event_veto` therefore vetoes only when a colliding source's emitters are equal **or unresolved** — unresolved FAILS CLOSED, matching `is_authoritative_source`'s own default, so we loosen only on a difference we can actually establish. Different emitters merely lift the veto; the ordinary layers then score the pair on its merits. `_collision_sources` is the fact ("distinct refs under a shared namespace"), `_same_source_event_veto` is the decision, and **all three enforcement points read the decision** — the engine (`_classify`), the merge choke (`ledger.merge_transactions` F23(a)) and the post-LLM re-check (`match_review`); they drifted once and a test now sweeps `backend/` for any direct read of the bare fact. The emitter lives on the ref (`external_refs[].emitter`), resolved SERVER-SIDE at both ledger write chokes from `email_snapshots.from_addr` (`services.email_snapshots.emitter_for`) — never caller-supplied, because the model supplies `external_refs` and must not be able to assert who sent an email. Best-effort: an unresolved emitter is a real state (the key is omitted, not stored as None), and a lookup failure must never fail a transaction write. The scars: one S$180.00 Meridian→Union card payment booked twice (bank emailed on send, card issuer on receipt) and one S$24.40 Ridgeline Mobile charge booked twice two automation runs apart (Harborview Bank's card alert, Ridgeline Mobile's merchant receipt) — in both the engine did not score the pair low, it never scored it at all. Measured on the live book the fix took auto-merge-eligible pairs from 5 to 0 while surfacing the Ridgeline Mobile pair at 0.75 for review; a blanket `email` exemption was rejected twice because 16 of 18 high-scoring pairs are same-emitter and every one is a real, distinct transaction, which under `dedup_policy="auto_high"` + L3 would be a silent merge of two real charges. KNOWN GAP: non-email namespaces have no emitter resolver yet, so they fail closed on every distinct ref, and the ~85 email refs whose snapshot was never stashed or has been pruned keep the old behaviour.
- **A deleted identity is REMEMBERED, and every create choke refuses it outside the `force` gate** (ADR `2026-09-02-a-deleted-identity-is-remembered`, superseding the unbuilt `2026-08-04-a-rejected-import-identity-is-remembered`). Every dedup layer reads LIVE rows only (`by_external_ref` excludes deleted rows by design, the L0 floors and the tool pre-flight sit on it), so a soft delete records that a row is gone but not that the owner MEANT it — and the next delivery of the same event books it again. `ledger.bulk_delete_transactions` (and `delete_transactions_where` for every filter except `batch_id`) writes one `rejected_identities` record per movement-scoped identity `(source, ref, from_account_id, to_account_id)` — the scope `matching._batch_l0` uses, because one Wise email books two legs on two routes and deleting one must not reject the other — inside the delete's own unit of work (`services/rejected_identities.remember_deleted`). `create_transaction` and `bulk_create_transactions` consult it AFTER L0 and the semantic guard and REGARDLESS of `force` / `dedup_by_external_ref` / `allow_duplicate`: skipping duplicate detection and overriding the owner's own delete are two consents, and a flag granting the first must never silently grant the second. A live row holding the ref still wins as idempotent `already_recorded`; a rejection fires only when no live row holds it; `update_transaction`'s inner rebuild bypasses it (a stale record must never refuse an edit). The outcome is its own word, never silent: `PreviouslyDeletedError` (409 `previously_deleted`) at the service; per-row status `previously_deleted` + a `previously_deleted` list + a NOT-BOOKED note on `add_transactions` (excluded from `undecided_matches` — there is no decision to make); `refused_duplicates[i].previously_deleted` on a statement commit; REST 409 with `retry_with: {resurrect: true}`. The ONLY override is an explicit consent read OUTSIDE the force gate — `TransactionCreate.resurrect` or a `decisions: [{index, action: "create"}]` on the tool — which books the row, stamps `meta.resurrected_from`, and forgets the record in the same unit of work; deleting it again remembers it again. Forget paths: `GET|DELETE /api/matches/rejected[/{id}]`, and un-deleting a row (`decisions._restore_deleted`). **Rollback ≠ reject**: batch rollback (`bulk_soft_delete`, `delete_transactions_where(batch_id=…)`) writes NOTHING — undoing a bad column mapping must leave the statement re-importable; trade undo (`equity`/`lots`) composes the delete with `reject_identities=False` (a guard test sweeps both files); merge tombstones (`merged_into`) are skipped because the ref legitimately lives on the canonical row. The standalone ledger lock set is `(transactions, txn_audit, rejected_identities)`; a composed caller already holding the first two passes `reject_identities=False` rather than re-entering the lock. Checkpoint-included + portable (`PORTABLE_FILES`/`BRIDGED`) — a restore that dropped the records would resurrect every deleted row at once. NO backfill: the 224 already-deleted ref'd rows on the live book are not remembered, because history cannot say which were rollbacks. The scar: a voided charge deleted by hand and re-logged by the next automation run under the same `email:` ref, with the run's own pre-flight reporting it unmatched. ACCEPTED COST: deleting a mis-logged row and re-importing must now be asked for, not assumed. KNOWN GAPS: statement preview does not flag a previously-deleted row before commit (it surfaces in the commit outcome), and there is no settings surface for the rejected list yet.

- **An identical row at a statement page boundary is a real charge, never a boundary artifact — the parse never dedups across pages** (ADR `2026-09-08-an-identical-row-at-a-page-boundary-is-a-real-charge`). - **Tool results are BOUNDED AT THE TOOL** (ADR `2026-07-19-bound-tool-results-filter-first`; helpers in `ai/tools/_bounded.py`) — tool_results were 48% of transcript bytes, and the supersession shrink missed 83 of 144 oversized results because it only fires when the SAME tool is called again. The first caveat fired on the very first end-to-end run against real data (`SELECT SUM(base_amount) … GROUP BY month` returned floats, because SQLite silently coerced the decimal TEXT to sum it); the second is the single likeliest way that tool produces a wrong answer. Do NOT extend this deviation to any OTHER money-bearing read: what makes it survivable is that SQL is self-evidently raw (so the caveat has something concrete to attach to) and that the tool is framed as discovery/analysis, never as a reporting surface. Scratch runs isolate with `ONW_ROOT` (or `DATA_DIR`+`CACHE_DIR`+`LOGS_DIR`) **and** `ONW_QA_MODE=1`, so a misspelled variable is a refusal, not a write.
- **A QA run (`ONW_QA_MODE`) schedules nothing that calls models, outside services or notification channels** — the owner's Automations, price checks/alerts, AI news and analysis scans, RSS, digests and the dividend refresh stay off unless the run opts in with `ONW_QA_RUN_SCHEDULES=1` (`scheduler._outward_jobs_allowed`, honoured by `start`, `reschedule` and `reschedule_digests`); local housekeeping still runs and no data changes (an Automation still shows active with its next run). The scar (2026-10-09): a QA clone of the owner's real book booted with 13 active Automations and monitoring on, fired a price alert within seconds, and with a model configured would have spent the owner's budget. Same rule as the boot price backfill and the CLI probe.
- **A file marked Private in Documents (internally the Vault) never reaches a model** (ADR `2026-09-26-vault-is-a-view-over-existing-stores`). Private lives on the attachment row (`vault.private`). Every path that hands attachment CONTENTS to a model reads through `services.attachments.get_attachment_for_model` or `get_attachment_import_source` — both answer None for a Private file — and asks `private_refusal` for the words to say instead: `read_documents` (via `services.vault.read_source`, which returns NO content for it), `read_attachments`, a re-attached ref on the current turn (`ai/attachments.py`), the import and equity-PDF parsers, `diff_statement`, and receipt promotion (a receipt is readable by `read_receipts`, so a Private file can't be copied into one). `get_attachment` itself is NOT guarded — the chat's own thumbnails read through it — so a new model-facing read must use the `_for_model` getter. Lucius may know the file EXISTS (it is listed, by title and with the user's note — the user's words, never the file's; its content-derived row `summary` is replaced while Private, restored from the parsed payload after); a receipt promoted from it is Private too (`read_receipts` refuses by `source_attachment_id`), a statement imported from it earlier is not; only the user's own edit switches Private off (`keep_attachment`, what `save_to_documents` calls, refuses to), and un-keeping a file leaves the flag set. Not a sandbox: a conversation that read the file before it was marked keeps what it read, and opt-in code execution is not a filesystem jail.
- **An owned asset's value is its newest valuation; its purchase point is derived from `cost`/`cost_date` — never stored, never today's value, none for policies; identity edits never write marks; a loan secures at most one live asset; equity is derived from the linked liability account, never stored** (ADRs `2026-09-06-assets-tab-faithful-port`, `2026-09-10-remove-owned-asset-review-scheduling` and `2026-09-26-owned-asset-purchase-point-is-derived`). Stored `marks[]` hold valuations only; `services.owned_assets_valuation.series()` projects them plus the derived purchase point (`id: "purchase"`, only when `cost > 0`, `cost_date` set and the category isn't `policy`, and only when dated on/before the newest valuation), newest-first by `(date, kind rank — valuation outranks purchase —, created_at, id)`, so today's value is PROVABLY a recorded valuation and no Purchase edit, category change or migration can move today's net worth. From 2026-09-26 no valuation may predate `cost_date`, a changed `cost_date` may not fall after the earliest valuation or the sale, and a sale may not predate the purchase (legacy violations stay editable). Recording appends; correcting (`correct_mark`) replaces value/date/note and stamps `edited_at`; removing (`remove_mark`) deletes — neither touches the purchase point, and the last valuation can't be removed. `share_pct` outside 0–100 is refused, never clamped. Disposal clears `loan_account_id`. A house, a car, a watch, a bar of gold — anything in `owned_assets` — has no stored "current value" or "balance" field: `marks[]` is the ordered value history (newest-first by `(date, created_at)`), and `services.owned_assets_valuation.value_as_of(asset, as_of)` (the newest mark on/before `as_of`, or the newest of all when `as_of` is None) is the ONE place that answers "what is this worth" — an asset's marks ARE its price series, so even a net-worth-history reconstruction values a past date honestly off the mark that was current then, never today's mark back-projected. `OwnedAssetsStore.update_asset`'s edit whitelist structurally excludes `marks` and every disposal field — renaming an asset, fixing its cost, or re-categorizing it can never move its value, mirroring the ledger's own "a stored balance is never trusted, a fold always is" posture one layer down. Insurance and maturity are typed identity fields (`insured: bool`, `maturity_date: YYYY-MM-DD | null`); asset review cadence, freshness projections, and linked `asset_remark:*` Automations are retired and rejected outside the one-time migration/import compatibility boundary. Equity (value minus what's owed) is likewise never stored: `loan_account_id` is an opaque pointer at a real liability account, and its balance is read live off the ledger (`ledger_service.account_with_balance`) every time — paying down the mortgage or refinancing the loan needs zero coordination with the asset row. `apply_owned_asset_change`'s `dispose` op is the one write that DOES touch the ledger: it posts a single-leg `kind="investment"` proceeds transaction and archives the asset in ONE `locked_unit_of_work`, with an `external_refs` idempotency ref so a retried dispose can never double-book the sale.

- **A database AHEAD of this checkout's code, BEHIND but held open by another process, or populated with no `alembic_version` stamp, is refused untouched — never backed up, never restored** (ADR `2026-09-06-database-ahead-of-code-is-refused-untouched`) — `_pending()` alone cannot tell any of those apart from ordinary pending work, which is how a stale checkout's boot once "restored" a live newer server's database it could not understand, unlinking that server's real, open `-wal`/`-shm` sidecars. `ensure_migrated` now refuses BEFORE any backup/`dispose_engine`/`run_upgrade` (`core.instance_lock.held_by_other`, no same-port sibling exception, gates every pending case; `_unknown_current_revision`'s exact revision-id match gates the sqlite-file case), and `_restore` itself writes IN PLACE via `sqlite3.Connection.backup()` — preserving the inode and every sidecar for a restore that legitimately runs — except the one narrow case that in-place restore cannot recover at all (the failed upgrade corrupted the destination's own header, `sqlite3.DatabaseError`), where it falls back to the old copy+replace path rather than leaving the database unrecoverable.

## Investments

- **A position's `quantity` / `cost_basis` / `cost_currency` are DERIVED from its records, with ONE writer** (ADR `2026-10-07-a-position-quantity-is-derived-from-its-records`). `services/position_figures.rederive(position_id, conn=)` is the only writer, and every choke calls it in its own unit of work. A grant's LOCKED position = Σ units of its not-yet-vested tranches (0 once the grant is cancelled). A grant's PENDING-RELEASE position = Σ units of its `pending_release` vest events, with basis from `equity_release.pending_basis`. Every other position = Σ `remaining_qty` of its open lots, with basis per the resync rule. The figures stay materialized so readers are unchanged, but `PositionStore.touch` refuses them; only `PositionStore.write_figures` (called solely from `position_figures`) writes them. Consequences: every holding has a record — an add books its lot (no date → an `opening` lot dated TODAY, tracking starts here; the user sets the real date on the lot later), and no read path ever writes a lot; a manual quantity/basis edit is refused with guidance (record a buy/sale/transfer, or fix the lot); `vesting_state="unvested"` positions exist only through the equity choke; a cancelled grant's unvested units are 0 (forfeited); a tranche edit keeps each tranche's vest state from the store (a vested tranche can't be resized or dropped until its vest is undone); a grant adopts only an unvested, lot-less, grant-less row; the one exception to derivation is a legacy unvested row no grant owns, whose stored figure is its only record and is left untouched. A grant's locked and pending-release positions hold NO lots — revision `202610111300_grant_holdings_hold_no_lots` removed the `opening` lots the old read seeded there, and boot reports any other (`position_figures.stray_grant_lots`). Returns price an `opening` lot with no funding transaction at its MARKET value on its date, never at its old cost (no money moved in the tracked period); a funded one is a purchase at cost. Parity (stored == derived) is asserted after every step of a random-writes property test, and `position_figures.reconcile()` re-derives any drift at boot and notifies the user — the migration logs it rather than refusing to boot. The scars: a lots READ route that seeded a placeholder lot on a grant (KSTL, 2026-10-07), a removed position whose lots stayed held, parked vests missing from lot rebuilds, and 23 lots left in settlement wallets by a position-only account move.
- **A sold lot is correctable; its sales keep their allocation** (ADR `2026-10-13-a-sold-lot-is-correctable-and-its-sales-keep-their-allocation`). A correction never re-runs FIFO or touches a sale's quantity, price or cash; only that lot's lines on each sale follow (date/term; gain when cost, fees or currency change, at the sale's stored rate). Its quantity can't go below what was sold. Purchase cash is rewritten only when a cash-bearing field actually changes.
- **A lot's `account_id` equals its live position's.** An account move (`update_position(account_id)`) re-files every lot of the position, open and closed, in the same transaction. The field stays stored (not joined) because fully-sold lots of a removed position outlive it as history.

## Budgeting

- **Ready to Assign follows YNAB: an envelope holds its own overspend until month-end** (ADR
  `2026-09-09-ready-to-assign-follows-ynab-envelopes-are-the-category`, restoring ADR
  `2026-07-02-balance-based-rta` + `2026-07-09-ynab-engine-credit-card-payment-envelopes`,
  superseding `2026-09-08-ready-to-assign-is-unassigned-cash` one day after it shipped):
  `available(m, env) = max(0, available(m−1, env)) + assigned(m, env) + activity(m, env)` — the
  in-month activity is SIGNED (an overspend counts negative) and only the CARRY into the next
  month clamps at 0; `RTA(m) = cash_as_of(m) − pre_budget_baseline − Σ_env available(m, env) −
  card_spend_not_covered(m)`, where the Σ is the SIGNED sum (not clamped per-envelope) — this is
  what makes a red envelope not move RTA relative to the same spend held by a non-overspent one:
  for a CASH overspend, cash already dropped and the now-negative `available` term offsets that
  same drop back out; for a CARD overspend, cash never moved at all and `card_spend_not_covered`
  cancels the drain instead (the card rule below). **Covering an overspent envelope now COSTS Ready to Assign**, dollar for dollar
  up to the hole's size — the opposite of what 2026-09-08 shipped for one day ("covering never
  changes it"): holding an overspend means someone still has to pay for it, and assigning money
  there is that payment; `_auto_cover_overspending` is capped at the displayed RTA (restored B4,
  symmetric with `_auto_underfunded`). At month-end, `max(0, prev)` clamps a still-negative
  envelope back to 0 — the hole is charged to NEXT month's RTA instead of carrying as debt
  forever; this is "held until month-end, then charged," not 2026-09-08's
  drain-immediately-with-no-reset. **Spend with NO bucket at all HOLDS THE SAME WAY** (ADR
  `2026-09-10-uncategorized-and-unlinked-cash-spend-holds-until-month-end`, amending 2026-09-09's
  "one fix," which drained it immediately on indirect research about YNAB — direct observation
  showed YNAB holds it in its own uncategorized pseudo-bucket): uncategorized CASH
  spend, and CASH spend in a category linked to no envelope, are each held by
  `held(m) = min(0, max(0, held(m−1)) + cash_activity(m))` — one instance for Uncategorized, one PER
  unlinked category, summed into the identity's own `held_outside_envelopes` term. That is
  `_available`'s recurrence with `assigned ≡ 0` (nothing can be assigned to a bucket that is not
  an envelope), so RTA does not move at the spend, the hole resets at the roll and is charged to
  next month exactly once, and labelling the transaction — or linking its category — before then
  makes the hold evaporate, the mirror of covering an overspent envelope. Collapsing the two
  no-bucket shapes to ONE rule (2026-09-09's actual insight) stands; the rule is the holding one.
  **The ONE asymmetry with `_available`** is the upper `min(0, …)` clamp: a no-bucket bucket holds
  HOLES but never a SURPLUS, because nothing can be assigned to it — so an inflow under an
  unlinked label (a categorized transfer arriving on-budget, an investment sell) is unassigned
  cash and still raises RTA, exactly as income landing on an unlinked category already did. With
  the value pinned ≤ 0, `max(0, held(m−1))` is provably 0, so the recursion collapses to the
  month's own activity and takes no prior-month walk. **"No bucket" is a CASH-only rule**, enforced
  structurally: the hold reads `_cash_activity_map` — filled by the SAME pass as `_activity_map`,
  but only from legs whose MOVING account is in `ctx["cash_ids"]`, the exact set `_cash_as_of`
  sums. Both sides of the identity therefore draw from one account universe. **The AMOUNT is
  cash's, not the envelope's**: the moving leg's OWN native figure at the CURRENT rates, the
  arithmetic `_cash_as_of` does on that same account — never a transfer's stamped `base_amount`,
  which `_transfer_base_value` takes from whichever leg was already in base currency and so is
  routinely the OTHER leg, at the bank's rate (ADR correction 3: the cap's pool mirrors ENVELOPE
  math, the hold's mirrors CASH math). A stored `base_amount` is read at all only while the row's
  `base_currency` still matches the current base — the standing `txn_base_amount` rule, which
  this branch had never applied. This is deliberately
  NOT the accumulator `_capped_card_credits` resolves its headroom against: that one admits every
  plan-source leg except a payment-tracked card's own OUTFLOW (expense, a categorized transfer's
  on-budget/moving leg, or an investment buy — LIVE or ARCHIVED alike, ADR
  `2026-09-28-iterative-available-walk-and-uniform-card-coverage`: every one of those is now a
  card-spend candidate, excluded from this accumulator whether or not a live payment envelope
  currently resolves), which still includes `credit` accounts the cash side never counts on the
  INBOUND side (an income refund landing on a card, a categorized transfer arriving at one).
  Sharing it fabricated Ready to Assign — see the correction in ADR
  `2026-09-10-uncategorized-and-unlinked-cash-spend-holds-until-month-end`. **The cash fold is
  NOT a subset of the full one**, and two consequences follow (both money bugs before they were
  fixed; ADR correction 2). An INFLOW on an unlinked category is in the cash fold and NOT in the
  full map (`fold_no_bucket_only`) — so a refund CANCELS the hold its own spend opened, without
  becoming Activity on an envelope that does not exist; a BLANK-category inflow is
  inflow-to-assign (`_INFLOW_CATEGORIES`) and folds nowhere, or salary would wipe the Uncategorized
  bucket's hold. And WHICH keys are evaluated at all (`_unbudgeted_keys`) is the UNION of both
  folds, never the full one alone: a categorized transfer or investment sell arriving at a
  `credit` account folds POSITIVE and can net a real cash hole to ~0 on the full map, dropping
  the key, its hold and its display row together. The cash half of that union is one-sided
  (only a NEGATIVE fold admits a key), so a lone inflow still mints no row.
  Card payment envelopes are automatic per card (ADR 2026-07-09): a funded swipe auto-moves the
  covered amount into the card's payment envelope (RTA-neutral); an uncovered remainder on a
  LINKED envelope is RTA-neutral too, via `card_spend_not_covered` (`_CARD_UNCOVERED_KEY`)
  cancelling that row's own drain; and a card spend with NO linked envelope is RTA-neutral by
  ABSENCE — no envelope row means no drain, so no term is yielded and nothing in the identity
  reflects the swipe (restored 2026-09-09 after a one-day regression yielded it unconditionally,
  making an unlinked card spend a bare subtraction that then evaporated at the next roll). Card
  neutrality is UNIFORM, with no linked-vs-unlinked distinction: unfunded card debt never
  manufactures assignable cash AND never reduces RTA — a card never had cash to lose, so its debt
  is never RTA's business. It surfaces only via the payment row's own `underfunded` figure, and
  touches RTA solely by being FUNDED: a user pays it down over time by assigning toward the
  payment envelope on their own schedule (the "pay a card off over time" capability 2026-09-08's
  continuous netting had no room for). **`RTA ≤ cash − baseline` is RETIRED as an invariant** —
  RTA can read above cash on hand in-month (a held overspend, resolved at the month-end sweep,
  not an instantaneous clamp). It is replaced by a closed-identity property: `RTA + Σ_env
  available(signed) + held_outside_envelopes + card_spend_not_covered + baseline == cash_as_of`,
  exactly, on every generated book (`tests/test_rta_identity_property.py`, which also pins
  `held_outside_envelopes ≤ 0`).
  The scar, and why it flipped twice in two days: on 2026-09-08 the owner's live book read RTA
  S$44,115 against S$37,665 of cash, because the pre-2026-09-08 engine treated one event three
  different ways depending on where it landed — held if it fell in an overspent envelope, wrongly
  ALSO held if uncategorized, drained if it fell in a labeled-but-unlinked category or crossed to
  an off-budget account. ADR 2026-09-08 fixed that by draining every outflow from cash
  immediately with no reset — but that identity matched neither YNAB's nor Actual Budget's own
  documented behavior (both hold an overspend in-month and charge it at rollover) and left no
  room for paying a card down over time, since continuously-netted card debt read RTA permanently
  negative by the size of the old balance. ADR 2026-09-09 restores the pre-09-08 engine with the
  ONE genuine fix (Uncategorized stops being held) rather than inventing a fourth model — see both
  ADRs for the full comparison against YNAB/Actual.
- **`cash_overspending(m) ≥ 0`, always — the roll-forward charge the RTA popover shows never
  contains card-caused overspending** (ADR `2026-09-13-rta-popover-flow-framing`). Defined as
  `Σ_rows max(0, −available(m, row)) − card_spend_not_covered(m) − held_outside_envelopes(m)`,
  this is a THEOREM, not an assumption: for a spending row, `_capped_card_credits`'s own
  per-row identity gives `overspend_row − uncovered_row = max(0, −headroom_row) ≥ 0` (the card
  half cancels exactly, pointwise, before summing), and `held_outside_envelopes ≤ 0` only ever
  ADDS non-negative room back. It is why the popover's line is genuinely labelled "Cash"
  Overspending rather than just "Overspending" — a card's own debt is never charged to Ready to
  Assign at the roll, matching `card_spend_not_covered`'s existing RTA-neutrality invariant
  above. Pinned by `tests/test_rta_flow_terms_property.py`, which also proves (two non-circular
  oracles) that the popover's flow terms are an exact rearrangement of the stock identity, never
  a second, independently-computed figure that could drift from it.

## Accounts and cash

- **Seven account types are the only classification axis: `cash`, `fixed_deposit`, `investment`, `retirement`, `receivable`, `credit`, `loan`** (ADR `2026-09-07-seven-account-types-one-axis`, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-07-account-taxonomy-design.md`). Everything else the app asks of an account — `kind` (asset vs. liability), band, spendability, budget membership — is DERIVED from the type; `kind`, `asset_class`, `locked`, `on_budget`, and `group` no longer exist as stored fields, on `Account`/`AccountCreate`/`AccountUpdate`, or on the `create_account`/`update_accounts` tool schemas, and the store REFUSES any type outside the seven. The scar: 26 types (15 with zero behavior) plus five overlapping classification axes let `cash_balance_base` net a brokerage account's drawn margin into spendable cash, let a `pension` account with no `asset_class` band as `cash`, and left "cash on hand" reading three different numbers on Accounts / Assets / Plan → Cash Flow for the same book — the owner had to ask which one was true.
- **Cash on hand = Σ `cash` — one function, one number, in every surface**: the Accounts sidebar total, the Assets "Cash & Savings" headline, Plan → Cash Flow's "Cash on hand · today", and the AI snapshot all read the same fold. Settlement cash — including a drawn margin balance — belongs to `investment`, never netted into `cash`: a margin debit is a loan secured by securities and follows its collateral the way a mortgage follows the property, exactly as a broker's own net-liquidation figure treats it. It enters the runway calculation only through the Investments toggle (positions net of settlement cash), never through the cash baseline.
- **Dashboard net worth includes retirement accounts in full.** The Assets breakdown assigns a retirement account's cash and vested positions to Retirement, removing those positions from its Investments card without changing net worth. `retirement_base` and `net_worth_excl_retirement` include both cash and vested positions; the latter is an account-scope comparison, not a measure of spendable money, and is not shown under the Dashboard headline.
- **On-budget = `cash` + `credit`, with no override.** `is_on_budget(a) ≡ a.type in {"cash", "credit"}` — every `credit` account (not just `credit_card`, widening ADR `2026-07-09`) is payment-tracked; `investment` is off-budget by convention (YNAB/Actual). `fixed_deposit` is deliberately the one type without a peer precedent: peers don't compute runway, so a locked deposit counted as spendable would overstate it, and a type (visible in the picker and the band) beats a hidden `locked` flag — hidden axes were the complaint in the first place.
